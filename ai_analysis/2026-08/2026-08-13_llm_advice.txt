### 4. Stocks to Keep an Eye On:

*   **Amazon (AMZN):** Billionaire David Tepper doubled his stake to $900M, making it his largest holding, betting on AWS reacceleration and custom silicon. However, insiders (Jeff Bezos, Andy Jassy) sold shares, and free cash flow collapsed due to $200 billion in 2026 CapEx, creating a "capex digestion phase" that requires patience from investors.
*   **AMD:** While Tepper cut his stake, its Data Center revenue more than doubled. It's also mentioned in the context of Trump's potentially conflicted trades, which could bring negative attention. Its high P/E (176) makes Intel's (80) seem reasonable, suggesting it's priced for high growth.
*   **Boeing:** Came under scrutiny due to former President Trump's stock trades, with lawmakers highlighting purchases before "favorable" statements or government announcements related to the company. The ethical implications are worth watching.
*   **CoreWeave:** Showed strong Q3 beat but tempered guidance led to a 16% stock drop due to a delay in capacity handoff to a single customer. This highlights the sensitivity to execution and major customer dependencies in the AI infrastructure space.
*   **Google (Alphabet):** Identified as a primary benefactor in digital advertising ("big walled garden") with an increasingly powerful data advantage and AI-driven optimization. However, Wall Street is alarmed by its expected CAPEX investments for the AI arms race, creating a potential headwind.
*   **Intel (INTC):** Posted its strongest revenue growth in 15 years, driven by AI and Data Center, validated by NVIDIA's $5 billion equity investment. Xeon 6 is its fastest-ramping product. However, its Foundry business burned a $2.1 billion operating loss, and a proposed $15 billion stock offering might weigh on shares. The path towards its price target depends on execution and securing 14A anchor customers.
*   **Meta Platforms:** While Ackman invested and it has an AI content partnership with Newsmax, its Reality Labs segment showed "visible cracks" with higher capex guidance causing a stock drop. It's described as "the worst AI exposure" due to metaverse spending. Its strong position in digital advertising is offset by concerns over AI investment.
*   **Microsoft:** Billionaire Ackman made a new investment, flagging it as a major AI winner, and analysts suggest its shares "could stay on fire." However, it announced job cuts (7% of workforce) amid heavy AI bets, and it's identified as a hyperscaler facing a potential AI funding crisis.
*   **Nielsen:** Despite DoubleVerify's weaker quarter, Nielsen's intended purchase is seen as a strategic move to create an "omni-channel measurement layer," which could lead to a stronger equity story and an eventual return to public markets. This acquisition-driven potential makes it interesting.
*   **NVIDIA (NVDA):** While it had a "massive beat on revenue and earnings," there are questions around inventory growth exceeding sales and insiders liquidating inflated stock. It's also mentioned in Trump's potentially conflicted trades and is central to the "AI capex overbuild" concerns raised in News 13, where vendor financing is described as a "deceptive form of equity financing." Its stock rallied then crashed, highlighting volatility.
*   **Oracle:** Raised a massive $38 billion debt package for data center projects and may need to borrow up to $100 billion more, leading to increased credit risk. This aggressive leverage for AI infrastructure is a significant watch item.
*   **Super Micro Computer (SMCI):** Surged 10% on strong FY27 revenue guidance that beat Wall Street estimates. However, it faces significant red flags including negative operating cash flow, persistent accounting issues (with federal prosecutors charging three individuals), margin volatility, and an ongoing independent review by its board. The balance between strong AI-driven growth and these operational/governance risks makes it highly speculative.
